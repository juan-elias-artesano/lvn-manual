1. Introduction & Philosophy

1.1 What is Liquidity & Volume Nexus?

Liquidity & Volume Nexus is a professional cTrader overlay indicator that unifies three institutional trading disciplines into one coherent system — and then does what almost no indicator dares to do: it measures its own signals and shows you the verdict.

  • Smart-Money Structure — fractal swings, Break of Structure (BOS), Change of Character (CHoCH), breaker blocks, fair value gaps and their inverses, premium/discount ranges with the Optimal Trade Entry zone.
  • Liquidity Mapping — Equal Highs / Equal Lows clusters, single-swing Buy-Side / Sell-Side liquidity, sweep detection with the raid's exact reach, prior-day and prior-week extremes — the resting orders every session trades against.
  • Volume Intelligence — a time-of-day-aware volume baseline, a per-zone Volume Score with exponential decay, a Volume Squeeze detector and a full session Volume Profile with POC / VAH / VAL.

On top of that map sits a signal engine: a Nexus Signal fires only when a strong volume-backed zone, a structural confirmation (or a freshly swept liquidity pool), a cost-aware trade plan, the session clock and the volatility regime all agree. Each signal becomes a projected trade plan (Entry / Stop / TP1 / TP2 / TP3), a corner card with a grade and trade advice, and an entry in a journal that records what actually happened — in R, on your broker feed, with profit factor, drawdown and a statistical-significance verdict.

1.2 The philosophy in one paragraph

No probabilities. No promises. Only measured facts. Every number this product prints comes either from live chart data or from its own journal of your feed. A grade (A+/A/B) is a deterministic checklist result, not a prediction. The journal tells you — with sample sizes — whether that checklist has been working on your symbol, your timeframe, your broker. The product places no orders, reads no account money and asks for no permissions (AccessRights.None).

2. Architecture & Guarantees

2.1 The closed-bar pipeline (why it never repaints)

cTrader calls an indicator's Calculate() on every tick, and the bar it passes is the one still forming. Nexus never lets an unfinished bar touch its engines: the whole pipeline (swings → structure → zones → liquidity → volume → signals → journal) consumes the bar that has just closed. The forming bar is used only for the live panel read-out, the provisional swing preview (explicitly marked, off by default) and the intrabar life-cycle watch (so a stop taken mid-bar is reported mid-bar — and latched: a wick that retracts can never "un-stop" a trade).

This was verified tick-by-tick: running the same series once as a live tick stream and once as closed bars produces identical journals — the same signals, the same fills, the same outcomes. What you backtest by eye on history is exactly what the live edge will do.

3. Swing Detection (Module 1.1)

Swings are the atoms of the whole system: structure breaks, liquidity pools and the dealing range are all built from them. A fractal swing is a bar whose high (or low) is strictly beyond its Swing Period neighbours on both sides.

  • Strict publication. A swing at bar C is published only after bar C + Legs has closed. Until then it does not exist for any module — this is the anti-repaint contract.
  • Tick epsilon. A neighbour must sit at least half a tick beyond the centre to count as lower/higher. Exact ties therefore cancel the swing — deliberately: equal highs/lows belong to the Liquidity Engine (Module 2), not to the swing list, so the same level is never owned twice.
  • Markers. A confirmed swing high draws a filled red ▼ above the bar; a swing low draws a filled green ▲ below it (colours configurable). Optional price labels print the exact level.
  • Provisional preview (off by default): a hollow △/▽ glyph on the forming bar shows a swing that would confirm if the remaining bars cooperate. It is repainting by definition, is drawn in its own grey colour, never influences any module, and vanishes the moment it becomes invalid — a confirmed swing never carries two glyphs.
Tip Swing Period (fractal legs) = 5 (default) is the balanced choice. Lower legs (2–3) mark up every micro-swing — good for scalping M1/M5, noisy elsewhere. Higher legs (8–12) keep only major pivots — right for H4/D1 swing reading. The same parameter family drives the MTF engine separately (HTF Swing Period).

4. Market Structure — BOS / CHoCH / Breakers (Module 1.2)

The Structure Engine tracks the active (last unbroken) swing high and swing low — the two levels the market is currently "answering to" — and publishes an event the moment one of them breaks:

EventMeaningOn the chart
BOS (Break of Structure)The break continues the prevailing context: a high broken while the context is Bullish or neutral, a low broken while BearishSolid horizontal line at the broken level, starting at the swing, extending past the break bar; label "BOS" (optionally with the price)
CHoCH (Change of Character)The first break against the prevailing context — the earliest structural hint that the trend may be turningDotted line, aqua when bullish (breaking a high in a Bearish context), orange when bearish
  • Break Confirmation parameter: Close (default — the bar must close beyond the level; fewer, stronger events) or Wick (any trade beyond the level; faster, noisier — one bar can then publish two events when it takes out both sides).
  • Synthesised context. After every event the engine holds a context reading — BULLISH / BEARISH / RANGE — shown in the badge, the panel, and used by the signal engine (the reversal setup is a Range-context specialist). Before the first event the context is RANGE.
  • Active BSL / SSL lines. Two dotted silver horizontal lines mark the current unbroken swing high (buy-side liquidity resting above it) and swing low (sell-side below). When a line breaks, the engine falls back to the previous unbroken swing of that side — the levels walk backwards through history honestly.
  • Breaker blocks. When a swing breaks, the engine searches back (window: Breaker Search Window) for the last opposite candle before the broken swing — the down-candle before a broken high (bullish breaker, drawn lime), the up-candle before a broken low (bearish breaker, red). That candle's full range becomes a zone: institutions that were trapped there often defend it on the retest. Near-duplicate breakers (80%+ overlap of the smaller range) are collapsed into one. Breakers age out after Breaker Max Age bars or when fully mitigated.
  • Swing separation. A liquidity sweep (Module 2, wick-based by default) never marks a swing as structurally broken (close-based by default) — so a wick raid below a low does not blind the engine to the real close-break that follows. This separation is what makes the BOS/CHoCH stream trustworthy on raid-heavy charts.

5. Fair Value Gaps, Inverse FVG, Mitigation (Module 1.3)

A Fair Value Gap is a three-bar imbalance: a bullish FVG exists when bar N's low trades strictly above bar N−2's high (the market jumped and left an unfilled range between); a bearish FVG is the mirror. The gap is drawn as a rectangle from the moment it forms, and from then on it lives a measurable life:

  • Size filter. Gaps smaller than FVG Min Size (x ATR) (default 0.25) or a points minimum are ignored — sub-noise gaps never become zones.
  • Partial mitigation. Every trade inside the gap updates its MitigationRatio (0→1). The fill's transparency follows the zone's Volume Score; a mitigated zone fades to a third of its alpha and its rectangle stops extending at the mitigation time (plus a few bars).
  • Full mitigation → Inverse FVG. When the bar closes through the far edge of the gap, the gap is consumed and — with Enable Inverse FVG on — an inverse zone (orange) is spawned over the same range with the flipped polarity: a fully filled bullish gap becomes resistance, a filled bearish gap becomes support. Inverses never flip again — no infinite ping-pong.
  • Lifetime. Unmitigated gaps deactivate after FVG Max Age bars; mitigated ones linger for Keep Mitigated Zones bars (for context) unless Hide Mitigated Zones removes them from sight. Deactivation is state, not deletion: an aged zone stops scoring and can no longer produce signals.
  • Polarity. Bullish zones (bullish FVG, inverse of a bearish FVG, bullish breaker, EQL, SSL) are candidate support for longs; the mirror set serves shorts. The signal engine only ever reacts at a zone whose polarity matches the trade direction.

6. Premium / Discount / OTE (Module 1.4)

The dealing range is the last confirmed swing high / swing low pair — the battlefield institutions are currently working within. The module paints its upper half as Premium (red tint — expensive; favour selling) and its lower half as Discount (green tint — cheap; favour buying), with three dotted reference lines:

  • RANGE HIGH / RANGE LOW — the range boundaries, labelled with prices.
  • EQ 50% — equilibrium: the fair middle. Above it, longs are chasing; below it, shorts are chasing.
  • OTE 62 / 70.5 / 79 — the Optimal Trade Entry band (yellow): the 62%–79% retracement of the range measured from the top, where a continuation long ideally enters on a pullback. Shorts mirror it: their OTE is the same band measured from the bottom (the module draws the retracement of the current range direction).

The range redraws only when a new swing confirms or on the scheduled refresh — it is cheap, stable and never flickers. With Show Premium / Discount Range off, the whole module (including its eight chart objects) is removed cleanly.

7. Liquidity Engine & Sweeps (Module 2)

Liquidity is where resting orders (stop-losses, breakout buys) sit. The engine maintains two families of levels:

7.1 Equal Highs / Equal Lows (EQH / EQL)

  • Confirmed swings within Liquidity Lookback bars are clustered when their prices agree inside a tolerance (points or ATR-based). A cluster of ≥ Minimum Touches (default 2) swings becomes a pool: orange line for EQH (buy-side liquidity above), aqua for EQL (sell-side below).
  • The label shows touches ("EQH x3") and the zone's Volume Score. The more touches, the thicker/denser the line style — a triple-touch EQH is a magnet with a lot of stops behind it.
  • Clusters refresh in place with stable object names: a level that gains a third touch updates instead of redrawing somewhere else. A swing whose liquidity was already taken can never seed a new cluster — no phantom "fresh" levels out of consumed swings.

7.2 Single-swing BSL / SSL

  • The last unbroken swing high carries a BSL line (gold — buy-side resting above), the last unbroken swing low an SSL line (turquoise — sell-side below). They follow the newest confirmed swing automatically and live until swept — age never kills them, because "the market hasn't traded there for 300 bars" does not remove the orders resting there.

7.3 Sweeps — the event everything revolves around

  • A pool is swept when price trades beyond its level: by wick (Sweep Confirmation = Wick, default — raids are wicks) or by close. The engine records the sweep bar, the exact raid reach (that bar's extreme — the future reversal stop anchor), marks the source swings as swept (but not structurally broken), and repaints the level grey + dotted with a "SWEPT" label.
  • Swept pools stay visible for Keep Swept Pools bars (default 60; 0 hides them at once) — they are listed on the panel with a "*" marker, because a freshly swept level is exactly what the Reversal setup fades.
  • A sweep of buy-side liquidity above the highs is, in smart-money reading, fuel for a move down (stops of shorts were harvested, breakout buyers are trapped); a sweep of sell-side below the lows is fuel for a move up. The signal engine turns that reading into an actual setup — see Module 4.1.

8. Prior Day / Prior Week Levels (Module 2.5)

Four fixed lines every session trader needs: PDH / PDL (previous completed UTC day's high and low, solid) and PWH / PWL (previous completed ISO week's extremes, dotted, slightly transparent). Labels carry the prices; the panel prints all four with their signed distance from the live price in pips.

  • Immutable by construction. Levels are recomputed only when the closed bar crosses into a new day/week, and the scan never includes the current period — today's spike can never leak into "yesterday's high". They cannot repaint, period.
  • These are the highest-probability liquidity references on the chart: algos and desk traders key entries, stops and targets to PDH/PDL daily. A wick through PDH that closes back below it is one of the most reliable intraday fade patterns there is — and Nexus detects exactly that shape as a reversal signal when the raid coincides with a tracked pool.

9. Volume Nexus — Baseline & Zone Score (Module 3)

9.1 The honest yardstick: time-of-day baseline

cAlgo exposes tick volume only, and tick volume is strongly seasonal: the London open prints several times the volume of the late New York session. Comparing a bar against a plain rolling average therefore calls everything an anomaly at 08:00 and nothing at 20:00. Nexus maintains a per-time-bucket average (bucket size: Session Bucket Size, default 60 min) over the loaded history and blends it with the rolling average:

Baseline ModeYardstickUse it when…
RollingSimple average of the last N bars (v1 behaviour)24/7 instruments with flat seasonality (some crypto)
SessionAverage of the same time-of-day bucket onlyStrongly seasonal instruments (FX during sessions)
Blended (default, 60% session)Weighted mix of bothThe universal choice

A bar never inflates its own yardstick (the baseline for bar N is built strictly from bars before N), and Baseline History bounds the sliding window (5000 bars default; 0 = all loaded history). The panel prints all three numbers side by side — bar volume, baseline with its %, the rolling average and the same-hour average — so you can always see which question is being asked.

9.2 Zone Volume Score (0–100)

Every active zone accumulates the volume of the bars that trade through it. The score is (average per-bar volume inside the zone) / (current baseline) × 50, clamped to 0–100: 50 means the zone trades exactly at the market's normal rate; 100 means twice the rate or more. With Zone Score Half-Life (default 200 bars) contributions decay exponentially, so a zone is scored on what it has been doing lately, not on one busy bar from three weeks ago.

≥ 60 (Strong)Strong (thickness 2)Above-normal activity — a zone worth trading; the signal engine's default floor

The score label is printed inside each zone (optional) and the fill alpha scales with it: bright zones are busy zones.

10. Volume Squeeze Detector (Module 3.4)

A squeeze is a stretch of unusually quiet volume — the market coiling before expansion. The detector counts consecutive closed bars whose volume sits below Squeeze: Volume Below (% of the time-of-day baseline, default 60%). After Squeeze: Minimum Bars (default 5) such bars — and, when Require Zone Nearby is on, only within Zone Proximity pips of an active zone — the state fires once: a yellow SQZ marker appears under the bar, the badge and panel read "SQUEEZE", and (optionally) an alert is raised. The counter resets the first time volume returns to normal.

How to read it A squeeze next to a strong zone or a liquidity level is the highest-quality version of this marker: quiet acceptance against a level that keeps holding, right before the session brings volume back. A squeeze in the middle of nowhere is just lunchtime. See Section 2.10 for the playbook.

11. Volume Profile (Module 3.5)

A price-binned histogram of the selected period, drawn to the right of the last bar:

  • Range modes. Last Bars (rolling N bars, default 200) or true calendar periods — Current Day / Current Week / Current Month in UTC (not rolling 24h/7d windows — a daily profile never mixes two trading days).
  • POC (Point of Control, yellow line) — the price level with the most volume: the period's fair price and a magnet whenever price strays.
  • VAH / VAL (Value Area High/Low, aqua) — the band holding Value Area% (default 70) of the period's volume around the POC. Inside the VA the market is "accepting" price; outside it is exploring, and edges of the VA become support/resistance.
  • Each bar's volume is spread evenly across the bins its range covers; bins under 4% of the maximum are not drawn (the histogram stays a calm background column, not a neon block). The panel prints POC/VAH/VAL, the price's signed distance from the POC in pips, and whether price is inside or outside the VA.

13. The Signal Card & Grades (Module 4.4)

The card is a corner-locked static text block (bottom-left by default) that mirrors the most recent accepted signal — it is present right after you attach the indicator, follows its plan while the plan lives, then collapses into a compact RESULT card reporting the outcome. It is the product's dashboard at the moment of decision.

13.1 Grades — a checklist, not a prophecy

The four gates on the card: (1) zone score above the signal floor; (2) structure confirmation — a fresh matching BOS/CHoCH for continuations, or the SFP close itself for reversals (a reversal is never punished for lacking the very break that contradicts it); (3) the volume anomaly, judged on the same time-of-day baseline the engine used; (4) HTF context not opposing. Below the grade the card prints the grade's own track record on your feed — Track A: 12 closed, avg +0.31R, win 58% — so the letter is never read without its history.

13.2 Card modes & lifetime

  • Lifecycle (default): the card stays while the plan is actionable, carrying a live STATUS LIVE n bars left (or STATUS LIMIT PENDING @ price for an unfilled limit), then becomes the RESULT card for the configured hold.
  • Timer: the classic auto-hide countdown.
  • Always: never hides (not recommended — a dead plan keeps showing numbers).
  • A card restored from the history replay after attach / timeframe switch is tagged [history] and prints its true age ("95 bars ago") — an old outcome can never masquerade as breaking news. Countdowns arm on the first live tick, so a deep replay can never swallow the card before you see it.

14. Trade Plan Projection & Life-Cycle (Module 4.5)

14.1 The projection

From the signal bar the plan is drawn on the chart: a solid silver ENTRY line, a dotted red STOP line (with its distance in pips), TP1 (solid-ish), TP2 and a dimmer TP3 (each labelled with price and R multiple), plus translucent risk and reward boxes and a header with direction, grade and capped R:R ("1 : 5+" above the display cap — the raw value lives in the journal). The projection lives for Trade Plan Horizon bars (default 60) and is then removed automatically — no orphans, no manual cleanup.

14.2 Stop & target construction

  • Stop: for a long — the deepest of {zone bottom, active swing low, and for reversals the sweep extreme and the signal bar's low}, minus a 2-tick buffer; mirrored for shorts. If no structural reference exists on the risk side, the plan declares itself non-structural, reports R:R = unknown (0) and is rejected by the gates rather than printed with an absurd ratio.
  • Targets: the nearest and second- and third-nearest live (unswept) opposite-side liquidity pools, capped at Max Target Distance (x ATR) — a pool 300 pips away is real liquidity but not a first objective. Beyond the cap, volatility-scaled fallbacks take over (2× / 4× / 6× ATR), then labelled pip fallbacks. Every rung says what it is made of ("EQH x2 pool", "2x TP1", "fallback 4x ATR").

14.4 Market vs limit entries

  • Market at close (default): the entry is the signal bar's close — filled by definition.
  • Limit at zone EQ: the plan bids/asks the zone equilibrium for a pullback entry. The whole plan (stop, targets, R:R, size) is measured from the limit price. If the equilibrium sits on the wrong side of the close, the plan silently falls back to market with an honest note. While unfilled the card reads LIMIT PENDING; a bar that gaps through the limit fills at the better open price; TP1 is never booked on the fill bar itself (the intrabar order of touches is unknowable); a never-traded order ends NOT FILLED and contributes nothing to your statistics.

15. The Trade Advice Block (new in v2.3.0)

Three lines at the bottom of the card answer the questions a trader actually has at the moment of decision — every figure from live data or your own journal, never a probability: 

ADVICE ▲ LONG  signal 01 May 12:00 UTC (LDN)
  hold <= 60 bars  (~5h, ~5 H4 bars) - then exit or re-assess
  adverse: max 20p = 1.0R at the stop; typical MAE -0.42R (n=12)
  • (a) WHEN — the signal bar's time (UTC) and session.
  • (b) HOW LONG the premise lives — the plan horizon in bars, translated into wall-clock time using your series' own median bar step, and — with MTF on — into higher-timeframe bars, because "60 bars" is five hours on M5 and ten days on H4. Past that line the plan expires by design: exit or re-assess, do not "hold and hope". This is the number to consult when price goes against you and you are deciding whether the idea is merely early or simply wrong.
  • (d) HOW FAR price may travel against you — two honest figures: the contractual maximum (your stop distance in pips and R — beyond it there is no position, only history) and the empirical expectation: the average MAE of your feed's measured trades with its sample size. Adverse movement inside the typical MAE is the plan breathing; consistently beyond it means the entry class is worse than your record — that is data, not opinion.
  • (c) The ladder — TP1/TP2/TP3 and the stop live in the plan section directly above, each with its R multiple.

The block is switchable (Toast: Show Trade Advice); with it off, the classic card returns, including the one-line "When" stamp.

18. Multi-Timeframe Mode (Module 4.3)

With Enable Multi-Timeframe Mode on, an independent structure + zone stack runs on the chosen higher timeframe (default H4) over closed HTF bars only:

  • HTF zones render semi-transparent (HTF Transparency) and carry an [H4] tag in their labels — and in signal reasons, cards and the journal when a signal reacts at an HTF level.
  • The HTF context (Bullish / Bearish / Range) feeds the badge, the card's HTF line, the optional Require HTF Confirmation veto and the A+ grade, and arbitrates reversal setups.
  • HTF zones share the chart registry and accumulate volume on chart bars — they carry a real Volume Score measured at your chart's resolution. Rendering is owned exclusively by the MTF pass: no delete/redraw churn, and an HTF zone that ages out loses its objects in the same pass.
  • Grade A+ needs HTF alignment only when MTF is on — without a higher timeframe there is nothing to align to, and A+ stays reachable.

An optional board (off by default) listing up to Watch: Max Symbols symbols on a shared higher timeframe: each row shows the symbol's last structural context (BULL ▲ / BEAR ▼ / RANGE ◆ from its own swing-break scan), its current price, and the signed pip distance to its nearest resting BSL / SSL liquidity — where the neighbouring markets keep their stops. One throttled pass per refresh interval, no chart objects beyond one static text, no extra permissions. 20. Alerts, Position Sizing & the Cost Model

20.1 Alerts

  • Signal alert: the cTrader log receives [LVN ALERT] Nexus LONG | EURUSD M15 :: reason | price | time and (optionally) a platform notification sound. The indicator API has no popup channel — the log line and the corner card are the popup, and the sound is your eyes-free trigger.
  • Outcome alert: when a plan ends — STOPPED −1.0R after 5 bars, TP1 TAGGED +2.0R, NOT FILLED (with the honest reason, including the gap-through-stop rejection) — horizon expiry and supersede are treated as bookkeeping and stay silent.
  • Squeeze alert: optional, when a squeeze fires at the live edge.
  • All alerts are live-edge only: attaching the indicator to a deep history never machine-guns you with last month's signals.

20.2 Position size hint

With Risk Per Plan (% of balance) > 0 (default 1%), the card and panel print the suggested volume for the plan — computed by the platform's own proportional-risk arithmetic from the plan's stop distance, rounded to your symbol's volume step. It is display-only: the product places nothing and never reads your money amounts itself. An unavailable account simply prints no line — a wrong size would be worse than none.

20.3 The cost model

  • The spread is re-read from the feed on every closed bar; when a feed publishes none (some CFD/crypto symbols), a manual assumption wins, else a conservative instrument-class estimate applies (1 pip FX; 2 pips metals, indices and crypto — including the six-character crypto pairs). Anything assumed is flagged "(assumed)" on the panel so a cost figure is never mistaken for a quote.
  • Net R:R = (reward − spread) / (risk + spread): you pay the spread twice, in and out. The card prints both ratios (capped for display at Max Displayed R:R — above it you see "5+"; gates, journal and CSV keep the raw value), and the gates judge the net one.
  • Micro-stop gate: a plan whose entire risk is narrower than Minimum Plan Risk (x spread) (default 3) cannot be executed as printed and is rejected before anything is drawn, alerted or journaled.
  • v2.4.1 unit fix — re-run old backtests: the spread is a price difference (Ask−Bid); pre-2.4.1 builds read it as a point count and re-multiplied by TickSize, making every cost gate (net R:R, max spread, micro-stop floor) silently see a near-zero cost. If you measured plans on v2.4.0 or older, those numbers were optimistic — the gates now charge what a broker actually charges.

20.4 Signal Bridge — the robot feed (Module 4.8, new in v2.4.0)

The bridge is a one-way, file-based feed for the companion cBot Liquidity & Volume Nexus — AutoTrader: on every live closed bar the indicator writes one atomic JSON frame — schema version, heartbeat, the current plan, a journal snapshot and the rejection counters — to Documents/cAlgo/LVN/bridge/<SYMBOL>_<TF>.json (temp file + atomic move, so a polling robot never reads a half-written frame). The robot's Verify / Follow modes read that frame; the bridge is never the robot's only channel.

  • Default OFF, and honest about permissions: file I/O needs AccessRights.FileSystem. The store build ships None, so switching the bridge on without rebuilding logs the exact one-line attribute change once — then stays silent instead of spamming errors.
  • Measurement-neutral: with the bridge ON the engine's canonical dumps are byte-identical to OFF (proven by the rig). Publishing observes; it never decides.
  • Live-edge only: history replay publishes nothing — a backtest has no indicator instance, and a replayed frame would be a lie about "now".

26. Life of a Signal — From Close to Record

  1. Bar closes. The engine evaluates the closed bar through the ten gates. Most bars die early and are counted in Rejections.
  2. Signal fires. The plan is built (stop anchored to structure, ladder from live liquidity, cost checked), validated (a plan with a non-structural stop or a ratio below the gates is rolled back — the engine behaves as if it never fired), then: marker, projection, card, journal entry, alerts (live edge only).
  3. The plan lives. Every tick, the life-cycle watches: stop, TP1, premise (zone / structure / supersede) and horizon. The card counts down; the panel shows the live R state.
  4. The plan dies — once, latched. The outcome tag appears on the chart, the result card recaps it, the alert reports it, the journal records status + R + holding time + MFE/MAE.
  5. The record accumulates. Averages, PF, drawdown, streak, t-statistic and per-grade verdicts grow. When both A+ and A hold ≥ 10 measured closes, the grade check tells you whether the letters mean anything on your feed.
Limit plans, precisely A limit plan adds step 2.5: PENDING. Only the cancel rules run while it waits (horizon / superseded / structure flip / zone gone → NOT FILLED). It fills on the first bar that trades the price — a gap through fills at the better open; a gap that would open the position beyond its own stop cancels the order (NOT FILLED, with the reason). Measurement starts at the actual fill, and TP1 is never booked on the fill bar itself.

27. Entering a Trade — the Procedure

  1. Read the card top-down. Grade and Track (is this letter earning its keep on this feed?), Setup (continuation or fade — they are managed differently), Gates (4/4 or not), Ctx/HTF lines (regime agreement).
  2. Check the plan geometry. Stop anchored to something real (the note says what); TP1 at live liquidity; net R:R ≥ your floor; risk in pips you can stomach at the suggested size.
  3. Check the ADVICE. Signal time (is this fresh or 40 bars old?), hold window vs the time you can actually manage the position, adverse guidance vs your patience.
  4. Place the order manually. Market at the close price, or a limit at the plan's entry — the product places nothing, by design (AccessRights.None). Use the Size line for volume.
  5. Set your broker SL at the plan's stop. The measured statistics assume the stop is real. Then let the life-cycle inform you: card STATUS, panel open-R, outcome alerts.
The freshness rule A signal is an entry now, not later. If you find a card 20 bars old with price already halfway to TP1, the trade is gone — entering there changes the R:R you were shown into something the journal never measured. The ADVICE line's age and the STATUS countdown exist precisely to stop this.

28. Managing a Trade

28.1 The ladder policy

  • TP1 is the system's exit. The journal measures every plan as TP1-first — that is what the statistics you read describe. The simplest way to trade Nexus exactly "as measured" is: full exit at TP1.
  • TP2 / TP3 are runner objectives. If you scale out (e.g., 50% at TP1, stop to entry, rest toward TP2/TP3), you are running a different policy than the journal measures. That is legitimate — but judge it with the MFE column: the CSV's mfe_r tells you, per trade, how much room runners actually had on this feed.
  • The stop does not move against you. Widening a stop converts a measured −1R into an unmeasured loss. Moving it to entry after TP1 is fine — just know the journal keeps scoring the original policy.

28.2 Using the ADVICE numbers while the trade runs

  • Adverse excursion. Price drifting against you inside the typical MAE (from the ADVICE line) is the plan breathing — your own feed's history says trades routinely dip that deep before winning. Consistently beyond it, or beyond ~0.7–0.8R with the hold window half spent, the premise is failing earlier than your record says winners do: that is the discretionary cut the numbers support.
  • Hold window. "hold ≤ 60 bars (~5h, ~5 H4 bars) — then exit or re-assess" is the premise's shelf life. At expiry the product itself books the plan EXPIRED at the close. If you are still in, you are holding on hope, not on the setup — re-assess against fresh structure, not against your entry price.
  • Premise deaths are information. ZONE GONE / STRUCTURE FLIP outcomes mean the reason for the trade dissolved before the price verdict. Taking those exits manually (as the product books them) keeps your trading identical to your statistics.

28.3 What the outcome tag tells you afterwards

"✗ STOP −1.0R" and "✓ TP1 +2.0R" need no translation. The neutral tags are the interesting ones: a cluster of SUPERSEDED outcomes means your cooldown/horizon settings overlap signals (fine — the newer signal replaced the older plan); ZONE GONE shortly after entry on continuations usually marks zones that were already mostly filled — check the entry-location mode; EXPIRED with a large positive MFE says the market moved but never quite tagged TP1 — that is the runner-policy conversation, and the MFE/MAE averages are your evidence.

30. False Moves & Breakout Clues — the Interpretation Chapter

Liquidity reading is, at bottom, the study of two questions: was that move real, or was it harvesting? and is the market about to leave this range, and in which direction? This section is the field guide.

30.1 Anatomy of a false move (the sweep / SFP)

The reliable signature, in order of appearance:

  1. A level with obvious resting orders: EQH/EQL pool, PDH/PDL/PWH/PWL, the active BSL/SSL, a round number. The indicator labels all of these — the fuel is visible before the match.
  2. The raid: a fast push through the level, typically a single bar or two, on elevated volume. In Wick sweep mode the pool greys to "SWEPT" immediately; the raid's extreme is recorded.
  3. The failure to progress: the close comes back inside the level (the SFP). This is the tell. A breakout that is real closes beyond and holds; a harvest wicks beyond and returns.
  4. The consequence: everyone who entered on the "breakout" is trapped beyond the level; their stops now fuel the opposite move. A REVERSAL signal firing here is the product trading exactly this mechanism — with the stop beyond the raid extreme, i.e. beyond the trap.
Practical discriminators — sweep vs real break
  • Close location. Close back inside the level = sweep. Close beyond + the next bar also beyond = break.
  • Bar shape. Long wick, small body at the level = rejection. Full-body marquee closing through = acceptance.
  • What happened after. A real break retests the level from the other side and holds (the level flips: old resistance becomes support). A sweep never retests successfully — price just leaves.
  • Volume profile. Acceptance means volume building beyond the old VAH/VAL; a raid on a spike that prints no volume beyond the level is a probe, not a migration.
  • The engine's own verdict. Wick-mode sweep + close back inside = the reversal setup may fire. Close-mode confirmation = a BOS prints and the continuation logic owns the move. The two modes literally encode the two readings.

30.3 Breakout clues — when the range is about to end

  • Squeeze + location. SQZ fired while price sits compressed just under a heavy EQH or just above an EQL (or against PDH/PDL) — the coil is loaded against a specific wall. The expansion, when volume returns, has a first direction bias: away from the wall it could not break, or through it if the release bar closes beyond with ≥ 1.5–2× baseline volume.
  • Repeated defence with shrinking recoil. Each touch of a level makes a shallower pullback (visible as successive higher lows into an EQH): absorption. The level is being eaten from below — breaks under absorption tend to run.
  • Value-area migration. The profile's VAH/VAL of the current session pushing against the prior session's — price is being accepted progressively higher/lower. Breaks in the direction of migration have follow-through; breaks against it are usually fades.
  • Structure compression. Active BSL and SSL converging (both within a few pips on the panel) = a triangle of resting liquidity on both sides. Whichever goes first harvests the other side's stops — many desks wait for that first raid and trade the SFP if it fails, or the continuation if it holds.
  • HTF context turning. An H4 CHoCH while the chart TF is still ranging against it — the higher timeframe is early, and chart-TF continuation signals in the new HTF direction are the ones to prioritise from here on.
The breakout entry discipline The product does not chase breakouts — it trades reactions (at zones) and failures (fades). If you want to trade the break itself, make the market prove it first: close beyond + a successful retest-hold, then let the engine's next zone-touch signal (often at the freshly flipped level or its breaker) be your entry. You will miss the first 20% of the move and avoid the majority of the traps — a trade most professionals make happily.

35. The Golden Rules

The ten commandments of Nexus
  1. The card is the contract. Trade what it says — entry, stop, ladder, horizon. A trade you improvise away from the plan is a trade the journal cannot vouch for.
  2. Freshness beats beauty. An average signal taken on its bar beats a gorgeous one taken 20 bars late. Read the ADVICE age and the STATUS countdown before anything else.
  3. The stop is sacred. It sits beyond real structure (the card says which). Never widen it; the moment you do, you have left the measured system and entered an unmeasured opinion.
  4. Trade the setup you are configured for. Continuation and Reversal are different trades with different regimes. The Setup line exists so you never confuse them; the Ctx line tells you which one the market is currently paying for.
  5. Costs are part of the signal. Net R:R, max spread and the micro-stop gate are not bureaucracy — they are the difference between a plan and a donation.
  6. Let the journal reach significance before you scale. Until t ≥ 2 (or at least n ≥ 20–30 measured closes in the black), trade the minimum size that lets you collect data. The product prints its own verdict; obey it.
  7. One impulse, one trade. The cooldown exists because markets repeat themselves within a move. Re-entering "the same idea" three bars later is the same trade with worse geometry.
  8. Context over signal. A signal against a strongly trending HTF, in premium, on a fallback target is three yellow lights — no grade redeems that. The badge and the ADVICE block are part of the signal, not decoration.
  9. Volume is the lie detector. Moves without participation (below baseline) fail; raids on absurd spikes (>8×) are headlines. The engine filters both — respect the same rule when you override anything by hand.
  10. Review weekly, adjust monthly. Five minutes with the journal block (32.1) per week; parameter changes only after enough closes to justify them. A system tuned on three trades is a mood, not a method.

37. Risk Management with Nexus

  • Start from the Size line. It is the platform's own proportional-risk arithmetic on the plan's stop distance at your configured % — the single most execution-ready number on the card. 1% default is a professional starting point; 0.5% while the journal is young.
  • Respect the cost gates. Minimum NET R:R ≥ 1.0 and Minimum Plan Risk ≥ 3× spread are the floor of tradability. Raising them (net 1.2–1.5 on wide-spread instruments) improves the average outcome at the price of fewer signals — usually a trade worth making.
  • Use maxDD as your sizing mirror. The journal's max drawdown in R is what holding this system actually felt like on your feed. If 4R of drawdown would have made you quit, your % per trade is too high — that is arithmetic, not psychology.
  • Correlated exposure. The Watch board shows neighbouring symbols' regimes at a glance: three pairs all BULL ▲ on H4 means one dollar-risk trade wearing three costumes. Count the idea, not the tickets.
  • The losing streak will come. The "worst streak" statistic exists so the streak doesn't surprise you: at a 50–55% win rate, streaks of 4–6 losses are ordinary weather. Sizing is the only defence that works against weather.
  • Never average down against a plan. The stop beyond the raid extreme / swing is where the idea is proven wrong. Adding size beyond it converts a measured −1R into an unmeasured disaster — the one mistake no indicator can survive.

39. What to Follow — the 30-Day Onboarding Plan

  1. Days 1–3 — observe. Defaults on your 1–2 chosen charts, MTF on, Debug Log on. Take nothing. Read each card top-down when it appears; read the history-pass report; learn your rejections line's normal shape.
  2. Days 4–10 — paper the signals. Log every card you would take (a screenshot or a CSV note). Compare against what the journal books. You are calibrating your eyes against the machine's honesty.
  3. Days 11–20 — minimum size, live. 0.25–0.5% risk. Trade only green-light combinations (29.1) — expect 2–5 trades, not 20. Follow the plan mechanically: stop real, exit at TP1.
  4. Day 21 — first review. Journal block + CSV: does the shape match your paper week? MFE/MAE telling you your exits are early/late? Adjust ONE thing (horizon, entry mode, or size) — never five.
  5. Days 22–30 — steady state. Continue at minimum size until the measured count crosses ~20–30 closes. Only then let the t-statistic conversation decide whether to scale toward 1%.
Why this plan and not "start trading" Every measurement feature in this product — fill-verified limits, replay exclusion, significance verdicts — exists to make one sentence true: your statistics describe your trading. The onboarding plan is simply the fastest way to accumulate statistics that are already true.

40. Reading Grades & Track Records Correctly

  • A grade is a checklist, not a forecast. A+ = very busy zone + all gates + HTF agreement. It says the setup is well-formed, not that it wins. What it wins on your feed is the Track line's job.
  • Compare letters only within your feed. "A+ averages +0.52R here" and "A averages +0.14R" is actionable; someone else's A+ statistics are not your market.
  • The grade check sentence is a feature, not an insult. "A+ does NOT beat A on this feed (n=13/11)" means: on this symbol/TF/broker, the HTF-alignment and score bonuses add no measurable value — trade both letters equally, or investigate why (often: your HTF pair is wrong for this instrument — try a different Higher Timeframe).
  • Sample sizes rule everything. n=4 means nothing; n=15 is a hint; n=30+ is evidence. The panel prints n next to every number precisely so you never over-read a hot week.
  • Per-session insight via CSV: pivot result_r by the session column — many feeds show LDN and NY behaving differently; the session filter then becomes an evidence-based choice instead of folklore.

41. Performance & Chart Hygiene Tips

  • Attaching is cheap since v2.4.2: the history pass defers every draw nobody can see — structure events deeper than Render History (the surviving budget set is backfilled once at the window), intermediate zone and profile refreshes, unchanged active-level lines — measured 13× fewer chart operations on a 30 000-bar H4 replay (≈30 000 → ≈2 300), final chart state byte-identical, journal untouched. Load time barely depends on history depth any more. On weak machines you can still trim ink: Render History = 200–300, Zone Redraw Interval = 40, Panel Refresh = 2000 — signals and journal never change.
  • H4 + MTF configuration note: if your chart timeframe is the "Higher Timeframe" (H4 chart with HTF = H4), the MTF engine re-analyses the very same series — double work, zero new information, and both engines share one zone budget. Set the HTF one step above the chart (H4 chart → D1), or switch MTF off on that chart.
  • Clutter control without blinding the engine: Max Zones Drawn / Max Liquidity Lines Drawn cap what is painted, never what is evaluated. A clean chart with a full engine is the intended experience.
  • Timeframe switching is safe: open plans are journal-closed as abandoned (excluded from every average), objects are removed, and the history pass rebuilds everything. You will see the "[history]" card recap — that is the design, not a bug.
  • Baseline History: 5000 bars is the sweet spot; 0 (all loaded) is fine memory-wise since v2.0.0 and gives the most seasonal stability on symbols with months of history.
  • One instance per chart. Two instances of the product on the same chart would double every object family for no new information.
